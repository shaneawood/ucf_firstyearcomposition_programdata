What stands out across all four semesters

  • Modality shows the widest gap: 8.9% DFW in primarily classroom sections, 13.0% in hybrid, and 15.6% fully distance. Classroom sections had the lowest rate in every semester.
  • ENC 1101 runs lower in fall (7.6%, 9.3%) than in spring (16.8%, 11.1%), when its enrollment drops by more than half.
  • Students identified as Multi-racial (15.8%), Black/African American (14.6%), and Hispanic/Latino (13.9%) had higher DFW rates than White (10.8%) and Asian (10.5%) students.
  • Transfer students had the highest DFW rates (FCS transfer 19.7%) and the lowest persistence (86.7% to 87.8%, against 93.9% for FTIC students).
  • Fully distance sections enroll a larger share of Pell-eligible students (35% to 38% each semester) than classroom sections (24% to 27%), and persist at lower rates.
  • Persistence did not differ significantly by race/ethnicity or gender.

About the data and methods

Source

Institutional records for ENC 1101 and ENC 1102, Fall 2024 through Spring 2026: 11,891 enrollments and 1,422 DFW grades, reported in aggregate by semester, course, modality, student type, sex, and ethnicity. There are no section numbers in the data, so “course section” is shown as course and as course by modality. The gender view uses the institution's sex field.

Pell eligibility and persistence

Pell share is Pell-eligible enrollments divided by enrollments. Persistence is enrollments whose student enrolled in the next major term, divided by enrollments; two records (one enrollment each) with no persistence value are left out of that denominator. The institution reports these as counts within each group, not per student, so the data can't show DFW or persistence rates for Pell-eligible students specifically, or persistence for students who earned a DFW. Where Pell share and an outcome move together (as with modality), treat that as a question to investigate with student-level data, not a finding about Pell students.

Privacy

DFW rates are hidden (shown as —) for any group or cell with fewer than 10 enrollments. Rates for groups with 10 to 30 enrollments move a lot from a single grade and are best read alongside their counts. Nothing on this page identifies an individual student or instructor.

Reading the gaps

Differences between groups describe patterns in outcomes, not their causes. Modality, course timing, and student type overlap with one another and with the demographic groups, so a gap in one view can partly reflect another. The useful questions are about the program: which courses, formats, and supports are working for whom.

Statistical significance

A statistically significant gap is one larger than ordinary term-to-term variation would produce by chance. Tests are chi-square tests on outcome counts (DFW vs. not, persisted vs. not, Pell-eligible vs. not), pooled across all four semesters unless noted, with p-values adjusted for multiple comparisons (Holm). Effect size is Cramér's V; with this many enrollments, gaps of a few percentage points reach significance while still counting as negligible to small in effect-size terms. Those few points can still matter for a program.
